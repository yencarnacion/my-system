# Editorial record

The application uses the supplied 1929 Hereford edition. It preserves the original prose, including historical spelling and descriptive chess notation. Modern SAN is generated by legal replay; original move strings remain available in the laboratory.

## Source hierarchy

1. The supplied PDF scan is the visual reference, including errors printed in that edition.
2. The legacy hand-edited HTML supplies chapters, game structure, comments, and 237 diagram placements.
3. The supplied OCR text assists comparison; it is not treated as an infallible replacement for the scan.

`sources/book.html` is inert source material. Scripts, styles, external executable links, and a personal TODO comment have been removed. `sources/ocr.txt` retains the OCR text. The app does not serve either file as executable HTML.

## Machine-readable records

- `corrections.json`: initial scan-supported corrections and restoration notes.
- `audit-move-fixes.json`: individual descriptive-move corrections with evidence notes.
- `audit-additions.json`: score sequences restored from prose, damaged punctuation, and omitted score lines.
- `audit-diagram-fixes.json`: diagram placement/caption corrections supported by the scan or replayed game score.
- `chapter-lines.json`: 130 recovered chapter lines across 109 diagrams, with source references and research notes.
- `passage-links.json`: explicit links from 129 existing chapter lines to their source paragraphs; these are reviewed source associations, not nearest-diagram guesses.
- `passage-lines.json`: 43 additional prose sequences (616 half-moves), including examples without printed diagrams. Twenty-four verbatim comments are aligned to specific moves.
- `teaching-lines.json`: the opening chapter’s ten-move “pawn-less” example, with original notation and quotations separated from editorial learning notes and threat arrows.
- `import-report.json`: generated per-game completeness and diagram validity report.
- `public/data/book.json`: generated reader blocks, diagrams, annotations, and replay frames.

The compiler retains each source move string when a correction changes it, as well as an explanatory note. Some repairs are inferred from a unique legal continuation; the ledger distinguishes these from explicit scan readings. Legal consistency alone is not treated as proof of historical accuracy.

- Game 11: the diagram on p243 transposes Black’s king and g-pawn; corrected to Kg8 and Pg6.

- Diagrams with omitted pieces or incorrect captions were reconciled with the replay where the evidence supports a correction.

## Limits and further proofreading

All 50 printed mainlines replay legally. Several printed games end before the historical game’s final move; the app does not invent the missing continuation. Variations remain in the original comments unless separately recovered as a source-backed line.

Chapter lines add 1,742 half-moves. Together with game-linked diagrams, 150 of the 237 diagrams have replays. Each attached line must reach the diagram’s exact piece placement. The laboratory includes the surrounding original passage and labels research notes separately from book annotations. Short introductory teaching cues are editorial additions.

Diagrams CXXXIII and CLXIV remain open proofreading items: their supplied placements conflict with adjacent opening scores. Uncertain continuations were excluded rather than attached to a different position.

Pawn skeletons, split boards, frontier lines, and other schematic illustrations are not forced into legal positions. They retain their lines and piece arrangements. Standalone diagram placement does not establish historical castling or en-passant rights, and often does not establish the side to move. Manual exploration communicates that uncertainty and allows choosing the turn.

Printed page `p` lies on PDF spread `floor(p / 2) + 8`. The PDF links use this mapping. A scan spread includes two printed pages.

Further text proofreading, additional variation recovery, and historical result metadata can be added without replacing the full reader. Keep corrections in the ledgers and preserve published block IDs.

## Reading without mental replay

Passage players cover every main chapter and reuse existing diagram lines where the same moves occur in prose. They are an additional way to access those lines, not an independent count of unique moves. Every line stores its starting FEN and exact source blocks; a new legal replay verifies its frames. Comments labelled “From the book” must occur verbatim in the linked source paragraph. Automatically generated piece/square descriptions explain the move, while interpretive teaching notes are labelled editorial.

Coverage is not exhaustive. Unresolved or ambiguous continuations stay in the source prose. For example, the Chapter 1 Giuoco Piano passage replay stops at 7...Bxd2+: the next printed “Kt x B” does not establish which of two knights recaptures. The replay’s source note explains this endpoint. Diagram XIII’s caption-only continuation remains available from the diagram rather than being attached to an unrelated paragraph.
